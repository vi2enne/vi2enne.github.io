Postdoctoral Researcher · Aalto University

Vivienne H. Wang

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Aalto Robot Learning Lab, Finland, working with Joni Pajarinen. My recent research mainly explores novel algorithms for Reinforcement Learning and its applications in Agentic AI.

I did my PhD in the Robot Learning Lab of Aalto University and the Computer Vision Group of Tampere University, supervised by Joni Pajarinen and Joni Kämäräinen. During 2015–2016, I worked as a research assistant in the Deep Learning and Bayesian Modeling group of Aalto University, supervised by Tapani Raiko and Juha Karhunen, and I was a research intern at Nokia Research Center in 2014.

I have published at top AI/ML venues such as ICML, ICLR, AAAI and IJCAI, hold one US patent in the related fields, and received an IEEE best paper (runner-up) award at ICME 2015. My research publications in collaboration with Nokia scientists won the Nokia Labs Award in 2016 and 2017. I co-organized the RememberRL Workshop at CoRL 2025.

Research

My research sits at the intersection of reinforcement learning, probabilistic and generative modelling, and multi-agent systems, aiming to build agentic AI systems that can plan, act, remember, and cooperate reliably under uncertainty.

  • Reinforcement learning
  • Hierarchical planning
  • Probabilistic & generative modelling
  • Multi-agent systems

  1. ICML 2026 New

    Learning Multi-Timescale Abstractions for Hierarchical Combinatorial Planning

    V. H. Wang, T. Wang, J. Pajarinen

    The Forty-third International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026

    A model-based hierarchical reinforcement learning framework for sequential stochastic combinatorial optimization. A latent-space tree-search planner uses an SMDP-aware world model whose multi-timescale objective makes latent transitions reflect the duration of abstract actions, and a subgoal-conditioned budget policy allocates resources by context.

  8. CVIU 2016

    Primary Object Discovery and Segmentation in Videos via Graph-Based Transductive Inference

    H. Wang, T. Wang

    Computer Vision and Image Understanding (CVIU), 2016

    We present a novel algorithm that detects recurring primary object and learns cohort object proposals over space-time in video. Our core contribution is a graph transduction process that exploits both appearance cues learned from rudimentary detections of object-like regions, and the intrinsic structures within video data.
